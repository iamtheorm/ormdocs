A proof-to-production dossier on probability, estimation, calibration, dependence, numerical implementation, and test design.

01

Orientation / reading contract

The formula is the beginning, not the knowledge

A Bernoulli random variable is often introduced through two complementary event probabilities.

Pr⁡(Y=1)=pandPr⁡(Y=0)=1−p\Pr(Y=1)=p \qquad \text{and} \qquad \Pr(Y=0)=1-pPr(Y=1)=pandPr(Y=0)=1−p

That statement is correct and almost useless by itself. Quantitative work begins when the event must be defined without ambiguity, the probability must be estimated from finite and selected data, uncertainty must survive rare outcomes, and the estimate must remain valid after the environment changes.

This dossier follows one bit of credit information—default or no default—from mathematical definition to a production decision. Every layer asks a different question. Probability theory asks what the model means. Statistics asks what can be learned from observations. numerical computing asks whether the implementation preserves the mathematics. Validation asks whether the result transports. Decision theory asks whether an apparently small error changes money or risk.

The experiment contains 12,000 synthetic loans across 120 chronological cohorts. Development, validation, and forward holdout samples are separated before fitting. The final holdout contains both a worsening macro path and an omitted structural shift. This controlled failure is deliberate: a model that cannot be falsified teaches less than one that fails for a reason that can be located.

Central thesisA binary outcome is one bit of data. Extracting a defensible decision from it requires an entire chain of models and tests.

02 / Research object

What exactly is the random event?

Before writing a distribution, fix four objects: the entity, the event definition, the information date, and the horizon. Here the entity is an obligor, the event is a governed definition of default, the information set is frozen at origination, and the horizon is 12 months. Change any one of those objects and the probability refers to a different experiment.

The indicator records the realised event.

Y∈{0,1}Y\in\{0,1\}Y∈{0,1}

It is not the probability. The parameter describes uncertainty before the outcome is known. After the horizon closes, the indicator is either zero or one; the probability does not become zero or one retroactively. This separation between forecast and realisation is the first piece of statistical discipline.

Historical default frequency answers what happened to a group. Conditional probability of default answers what should be believed about an obligor given information F. A production PD system must connect those levels without leaking future information, confusing facility and obligor definitions, or treating incomplete observation windows as survival.

03 / Model boundary

Separate observation, parameter, and economic loss

For obligor i, the observed binary label and its conditional probability are distinct objects.

Yi∈{0,1}andpi=Pr⁡(Yi=1∣Fi)Y_i\in\{0,1\} \qquad \text{and} \qquad p_i=\Pr(Y_i=1\mid\mathcal F_i)Yi​∈{0,1}andpi​=Pr(Yi​=1∣Fi​)

A single Bernoulli statement makes no claim that two borrowers share the same probability or that their defaults are independent. Those stronger claims enter only when observations are aggregated or a likelihood is factorised.

Credit loss introduces two more random objects. Exposure at default measures how much is outstanding when default occurs; loss given default measures the fraction not recovered. The Bernoulli event activates loss, but does not determine severity. A perfect PD with inconsistent LGD or EAD definitions can still produce a wrong expected-loss number.

The dataset is split chronologically: 7,200 development loans, 1,200 validation loans for model and threshold choices, and 3,600 untouched forward-holdout loans. The split is part of the model specification. Randomly mixing future stressed cohorts into development would make the evaluation easier and the evidence weaker.

04 / Probability law

Derive the Bernoulli law instead of memorising it

Let D denote the event “default inside the horizon” and define the outcome as its indicator.

Y=1{D}Y=\mathbb{1}\{D\}Y=1{D}

If the event occurs, the indicator equals one; otherwise it equals zero. The two probability masses follow directly.

Pr⁡(Y=1)=pandPr⁡(Y=0)=1−p\Pr(Y=1)=p \qquad \text{and} \qquad \Pr(Y=0)=1-pPr(Y=1)=pandPr(Y=0)=1−p

The compact mass function is not magic.

f(y;p)=py(1−p)1−yf(y;p)=p^y(1-p)^{1-y}f(y;p)=py(1−p)1−y

Substituting one removes the second factor, while substituting zero removes the first.

Expectation becomes intuitive when the outcome is an indicator. Across many comparable repetitions, the average of zeros and ones is simply the fraction of ones.

E[Y]=p\mathbb E[Y]=pE[Y]=p

A binary indicator is unchanged when squared.

Y2=YY^2=YY2=Y

The variance therefore follows by subtraction.

Var⁡(Y)=E[Y2]−E[Y]2=p−p2=p(1−p)\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\mathbb E[Y^2]-\mathbb E[Y]^2=p-p^2=p(1-p)Var(Y)=E[Y2]−E[Y]2=p−p2=p(1−p)

Variance is largest at one-half and shrinks near certainty, but a small probability does not mean no uncertainty.

The moment-generating function contains every raw moment.

MY(t)=E[etY]=1−p+petM_Y(t)=\mathbb E[e^{tY}]=1-p+pe^tMY​(t)=E[etY]=1−p+pet

More importantly for modelling, Bernoulli is an exponential-family distribution. Its natural parameter is the log-odds.

η=log⁡ ⁣(p1−p)\eta=\log\!\left(\frac{p}{1-p}\right)η=log(1−pp​)

This re-expression produces the logistic link, a convex negative log-likelihood, and the bridge from elementary probability to modern binary prediction.

05 / Rare-event intuition

High accuracy can mean zero information

Take a representative 12-month probability of default.

p=7%p=7\%p=7%

Probability mass is radically asymmetric: 93% on survival and 7% on default. Predicting “survive” for every borrower therefore achieves about 93% accuracy. The model has learned nothing, but the metric looks impressive. This is the base-rate trap.

A useful credit model may be evaluated as a ranker, a probability forecast, and an economic input. Those are different jobs. Ranking asks whether riskier borrowers receive higher scores. Calibration asks whether predicted probabilities match frequencies. Economic evaluation asks whether the probabilities, severities, exposures, and costs produce better decisions.

The correct mental picture is not a bell curve with two bars. It is a probability measure concentrated on two outcomes, followed by a separate loss mechanism when the default state is activated.

06 / Portfolio aggregation

One Bernoulli loan does not make a Binomial portfolio

If n loans are independent and share one common probability, their default count is Binomial.

K=∑i=1nYi∼Binomial⁡(n,p)K=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_i\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p)K=i=1∑n​Yi​∼Binomial(n,p)

Its first two moments are immediate.

E[K]=npandVar⁡(K)=np(1−p)\mathbb E[K]=np \qquad \text{and} \qquad \operatorname{Var}(K)=np(1-p)E[K]=npandVar(K)=np(1−p)

Both homogeneity and independence are doing work. They are not consequences of the word Bernoulli.

Borrower-level models produce heterogeneous probabilities.

p1,…,pnp_1,\ldots,p_np1​,…,pn​

Under conditional independence, the count is Poisson–Binomial.

E[K]=∑i=1npiandVar⁡(K)=∑i=1npi(1−pi)\mathbb E[K]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_i \qquad \text{and} \qquad \operatorname{Var}(K)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_i(1-p_i)E[K]=i=1∑n​pi​andVar(K)=i=1∑n​pi​(1−pi​)

Replacing all individual probabilities with their average preserves expected defaults but generally changes the count distribution and every tail probability derived from it.

Dependence is a second layer. Shared macro, sector, funding, or collateral shocks make defaults arrive together. Marginal Bernoulli probabilities can remain correct while portfolio loss quantiles become disastrously wrong. Portfolio credit is therefore marginal PD plus a dependence model, not a larger Bernoulli formula.

07 / Estimation

Read the geometry of the likelihood

Suppose a rating grade is temporarily treated as IID Bernoulli. For an observed binary sequence with n observations and k defaults, the likelihood is a function of the candidate probability.

L(p)=pk(1−p)n−kL(p)=p^k(1-p)^{n-k}L(p)=pk(1−p)n−k

If only the aggregate count K is observed, its Binomial likelihood also contains the coefficient n choose k, which is constant in p and therefore does not change estimation. A likelihood is not the probability that the parameter is true: the data are fixed while candidate parameter values are compared.

Taking logs converts products into sums.

ℓ(p)=klog⁡p+(n−k)log⁡(1−p)\ell(p)=k\log p+(n-k)\log(1-p)ℓ(p)=klogp+(n−k)log(1−p)

Differentiation gives the score.

∂ℓ∂p=kp−n−k1−p\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial p}=\frac{k}{p}-\frac{n-k}{1-p}∂p∂ℓ​=pk​−1−pn−k​

Its curvature is strictly negative in the interior.

∂2ℓ∂p2=−kp2−n−k(1−p)2<0\frac{\partial^2\ell}{\partial p^2}=-\frac{k}{p^2}-\frac{n-k}{(1-p)^2}<0∂p2∂2ℓ​=−p2k​−(1−p)2n−k​<0

When 0 < k < n, setting the score to zero yields the unique maximum-likelihood estimator.

p^=kn\widehat p=\frac{k}{n}p​=nk​

When k = 0 or k = n, the likelihood is instead maximised at the boundary p = 0 or p = 1.

The development sample contains 476 defaults among 7,200 loans.

p^=4767,200=6.611%\widehat p=\frac{476}{7{,}200}=6.611\%p​=7,200476​=6.611%

That number estimates the average risk of the historical development mixture. The likelihood can be sharply peaked and still target the wrong future population. Precision conditional on a model is not protection against model shift.

For n IID observations, Fisher information accumulates linearly.

In(p)=np(1−p)\mathcal I_n(p)=\frac{n}{p(1-p)}In​(p)=p(1−p)n​

The corresponding variance lower bound for any unbiased estimator p̃ is inversely proportional to sample size.

Var⁡p(p~)≥p(1−p)n\operatorname{Var}_p(\widetilde p)\geq\frac{p(1-p)}{n}Varp​(p​)≥np(1−p)​

The sample rate later attains this bound with equality. Standard error therefore falls only at the square-root rate.

SE⁡(p^)∝n−1/2\operatorname{SE}(\widehat p)\propto n^{-1/2}SE(p​)∝n−1/2

To halve sampling error, approximately four times as many independent observations are required.

08 / Estimator anatomy

Why the sample default rate is an unusually complete estimator

An estimator is not justified merely because differentiating a likelihood produced it. We want to know which part of the data contains information about the parameter, whether the estimator targets the correct quantity on average, whether it converges as information accumulates, and whether any other unbiased estimator could have smaller variance.

For IID Bernoulli observations, the joint likelihood depends on the sample only through the number of defaults.

T(Y1,…,Yn)=∑i=1nYi=KT(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_i=KT(Y1​,…,Yn​)=i=1∑n​Yi​=K

The factorisation theorem therefore makes the count K sufficient for p: after K is known, the ordering of defaults and survivals contains no additional information about a common Bernoulli probability. Sufficiency is data compression relative to a model, not a claim that chronology or borrower identity is irrelevant in reality.

The Binomial count is also complete. If a function g has zero expectation for every interior probability, then the polynomial formed by the Binomial probabilities can be identically zero only when g is zero at every count. Because the sample rate is unbiased and is a function of this complete sufficient statistic, the Lehmann–Scheffé theorem makes it the unique uniformly minimum-variance unbiased estimator.

Unbiasedness follows immediately from linearity of expectation.

E[p^]=E ⁣[1n∑i=1nYi]=p\mathbb E[\widehat p]=\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_i\right]=pE[p​]=E[n1​i=1∑n​Yi​]=p

Its variance is the single-observation variance divided by sample size.

Var⁡(p^)=p(1−p)n\operatorname{Var}(\widehat p)=\frac{p(1-p)}{n}Var(p​)=np(1−p)​

Unbiasedness is a finite-sample statement about repeated datasets; it does not mean one realised estimate equals the truth.

Consistency is a different property. Chebyshev’s inequality makes the convergence mechanism explicit.

Pr⁡ ⁣(∣p^−p∣≥ε)≤p(1−p)nε2⟶0\Pr\!\left(|\widehat p-p|\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq\frac{p(1-p)}{n\varepsilon^2}\longrightarrow0Pr(∣p​−p∣≥ε)≤nε2p(1−p)​⟶0

Thus the estimator converges in probability, while the strong law gives almost-sure convergence. The central limit theorem adds its large-sample shape.

n (p^−p)⇒dN ⁣(0,p(1−p))\sqrt n\,(\widehat p-p)\xRightarrow{d}\mathcal N\!\left(0,p(1-p)\right)n​(p​−p)d​N(0,p(1−p))

These results require a stable data-generating probability and sufficiently weak dependence; more rows do not cure drift or clustering.

As an engineer, I learned here that a theorem is also a data-contract test. Consistency is not something the estimator carries into every dataset; it is something the sampling process grants when definitions, dependence, and the target distribution remain controlled. Before trusting a larger table, I should ask whether it adds independent information or merely repeats the same regime more densely.

The score for one observation is the forecast error scaled by Bernoulli variance.

U1(p)=Y−pp(1−p)U_1(p)=\frac{Y-p}{p(1-p)}U1​(p)=p(1−p)Y−p​

Squaring and averaging gives Fisher information.

I1(p)=E[U1(p)2]=1p(1−p)\mathcal I_1(p)=\mathbb E[U_1(p)^2]=\frac{1}{p(1-p)}I1​(p)=E[U1​(p)2]=p(1−p)1​

Independent information adds, so the sample contains n times as much.

In(p)=np(1−p)\mathcal I_n(p)=\frac{n}{p(1-p)}In​(p)=p(1−p)n​

For an unbiased estimator under regularity conditions, the Cramér–Rao lower bound is the reciprocal of Fisher information.

Var⁡p(p~)≥1In(p)=p(1−p)n\operatorname{Var}_p(\widetilde p)\geq\frac{1}{\mathcal I_n(p)}=\frac{p(1-p)}{n}Varp​(p​)≥In​(p)1​=np(1−p)​

The sample rate attains that bound exactly, so it is efficient for the interior IID Bernoulli model. At boundaries, with dependence, under selection, or when shrinkage is allowed, the theorem’s conditions or objective change. A biased Bayesian or hierarchical estimator can deliberately trade a small bias for lower mean-squared error.

09 / Statistical inference

Low-default data punish careless intervals

An estimate without an uncertainty statement invites false precision. The two-sided normal or Wald interval substitutes the estimated probability into its standard error and uses the positive standard-normal quantile z at cumulative probability 1 − α/2.

p^±z1−α/2p^(1−p^)n\widehat p\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\widehat p(1-\widehat p)}{n}}p​±z1−α/2​np​(1−p​)​​

It behaves reasonably in the centre with large samples and fails exactly where credit analysts often need it most: small samples and probabilities near zero.

With zero defaults among 40 loans, the Wald interval is [0,0], an absurd declaration of certainty. The upper endpoint of the two-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson interval is 8.81%, while the Wilson upper endpoint is 8.76%. A one-sided 95% exact upper bound would instead be 7.22%; tail convention is part of the result. No observed defaults means the sample contains weak evidence about a rare event, not proof that the event is impossible.

A confidence interval is a property of a repeated procedure: before sampling, 95% of intervals produced by the procedure cover the fixed parameter under the model. A Bayesian credible interval answers a different question after combining a prior with the likelihood. In low-default grades, defensible hierarchical pooling or external information can be valuable, but the prior must represent information rather than convenience.

Testing a rate change also requires power analysis. Under independence, 654 observations provide about 80% power for a shift from 6.0% to 8.5% in the experiment. Correlation reduces effective information, so an apparently large obligor count can still be a small statistical sample.

10 / Conditional probability

A portfolio average cannot rank borrowers

Real portfolios are heterogeneous. Logistic regression makes the Bernoulli parameter conditional.

pi=σ(β⊤xi)p_i=\sigma(\beta^\top x_i)pi​=σ(β⊤xi​)

The logistic function maps every real-valued score into the open probability interval.

σ(u)=11+e−u\sigma(u)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-u}}σ(u)=1+e−u1​

The log-odds become linear in the features.

log⁡ ⁣(pi1−pi)=β⊤xi\log\!\left(\frac{p_i}{1-p_i}\right)=\beta^\top x_ilog(1−pi​pi​​)=β⊤xi​

A one-unit coefficient change therefore has a multiplicative effect on odds rather than a constant effect on probability.

The independent Bernoulli log-likelihood becomes a sum over obligors, with the linear predictor ηᵢ defined explicitly.

ηi=β⊤xi,ℓ(β)=∑i=1n[yiηi−log⁡(1+eηi)]\eta_i=\beta^\top x_i,\qquad \ell(\beta)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[y_i\eta_i-\log(1+e^{\eta_i})\right]ηi​=β⊤xi​,ℓ(β)=i=1∑n​[yi​ηi​−log(1+eηi​)]

Its negative is binary cross-entropy. This explains why a machine-learning loss and a classical likelihood can be the same mathematical object viewed from different disciplines.

Ridge regularisation adds a quadratic penalty to the non-intercept coefficients β₋₀.

P(β−0)=λ2∥β−0∥22\mathcal P(\beta_{-0})=\frac{\lambda}{2}\lVert\beta_{-0}\rVert_2^2P(β−0​)=2λ​∥β−0​∥22​

Optimisation sees a penalty; Bayesian inference sees the negative log of a Gaussian prior on those coefficients. Both interpretations say that extreme slopes need stronger evidence, while the unpenalised intercept remains free to control the global probability level.

All feature transformations, imputation rules, and standardisation moments must be learned on development only. A statistically correct likelihood fitted with future-informed preprocessing is still a leaked experiment.

11 / Validation geometry

Ranking, calibration, and scoring answer different questions

AUC is the probability that a random defaulter receives a higher score than a random survivor. It is invariant to any strictly increasing transformation of the score. Consequently, multiplying all odds by a constant can leave AUC unchanged while corrupting every PD, expected-loss figure, and approval threshold.

Calibration asks whether forecasts keep their promises. Consider a group assigned a probability near ten percent.

q≈0.10⟹E[Y∣q]≈0.10q\approx0.10 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \mathbb E[Y\mid q]\approx0.10q≈0.10⟹E[Y∣q]≈0.10

Approximately 10% should default under the same definition and horizon. A reliability plot compares observed frequency with mean forecast across risk groups. Treating fitted forecasts qᵢ as fixed and assuming conditional independence, one aggregate calibration residual is standardised by forecast variance.

Z=∑i(Yi−qi)∑iqi(1−qi)Z=\frac{\sum_i(Y_i-q_i)}{\sqrt{\sum_i q_i(1-q_i)}}Z=∑i​qi​(1−qi​)​∑i​(Yi​−qi​)​

Parameter uncertainty, clustering, or model selection changes that reference distribution.

Proper scoring rules evaluate the entire probability forecast. The Brier score uses squared probability error.

Brier⁡=1n∑i=1n(Yi−qi)2\operatorname{Brier}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Y_i-q_i)^2Brier=n1​i=1∑n​(Yi​−qi​)2

Log loss penalises confident mistakes more strongly.

LogLoss⁡=−1n∑i=1n[Yilog⁡qi+(1−Yi)log⁡(1−qi)]\operatorname{LogLoss}=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[Y_i\log q_i+(1-Y_i)\log(1-q_i)\right]LogLoss=−n1​i=1∑n​[Yi​logqi​+(1−Yi​)log(1−qi​)]

Their expected value is minimised by reporting the true conditional probability. Accuracy does not have this truth-telling property.

The holdout result exposes the distinction. AUC remains 0.8155, yet mean predicted PD is 10.98% against an observed 13.25%. Ranking passes; probability level fails. A production decision that uses the probability must follow the calibration verdict, not the AUC headline.

As an engineer, I learned not to compress validation into one green metric. A system can order borrowers correctly and still misprice every decision because the probability scale has shifted. The monitoring interface, alert thresholds, and deployment gate should therefore preserve the distinction between ranking, calibration, and economic error instead of averaging them into one score.

12 / Economic translation

Probability becomes money through a loss mechanism

For one exposure, realised loss is activated by the default indicator.

L=Y×LGD×EADL=Y\times\mathrm{LGD}\times\mathrm{EAD}L=Y×LGD×EAD

The exact conditional expectation first conditions severity and exposure on default.

E[L∣F]=p E[LGD×EAD∣Y=1,F]\mathbb E[L\mid\mathcal F]=p\,\mathbb E[\mathrm{LGD}\times\mathrm{EAD}\mid Y=1,\mathcal F]E[L∣F]=pE[LGD×EAD∣Y=1,F]

The familiar product

E[L∣F]≈PD×LGD×EAD\mathbb E[L\mid\mathcal F]\approx\mathrm{PD}\times\mathrm{LGD}\times\mathrm{EAD}E[L∣F]≈PD×LGD×EAD

is obtained when the supplied LGD and EAD are compatible conditional forecasts—or when their product is treated as deterministic at the information date. If downturn LGD is combined with a benign point-in-time PD without an explicit scenario, the multiplication is numerically valid and conceptually incoherent.

Approval requires opportunity cost as well as expected loss. Let a performing loan earn margin m, default lose LGD, and operating cost equal c per unit exposure. Expected profit is then a probability-weighted payoff.

E[Profit∣p]=[(1−p)m−p LGD−c]EAD\mathbb E[\mathrm{Profit}\mid p]=\left[(1-p)m-p\,\mathrm{LGD}-c\right]\mathrm{EAD}E[Profit∣p]=[(1−p)m−pLGD−c]EAD

p⋆=m−cm+LGDp^\star=\frac{m-c}{m+\mathrm{LGD}}p⋆=m+LGDm−c​

Insert a 6% margin, 1% operating cost, and 45% loss given default.

p⋆=0.06−0.010.06+0.45=9.80%p^\star=\frac{0.06-0.01}{0.06+0.45}=9.80\%p⋆=0.06+0.450.06−0.01​=9.80%

13 / Dependence and tail risk

Marginal PD does not determine portfolio danger

Two portfolios can have identical borrower probabilities and identical expected loss but radically different extreme losses. In a one-factor Gaussian model, each obligor’s latent asset variable combines a shared standard-normal factor with idiosyncratic standard-normal noise; ρ_A denotes latent asset correlation.

Ai=ρA Z+1−ρA εi,Z,εi∼iidN(0,1)A_i=\sqrt{\rho_A}\,Z+\sqrt{1-\rho_A}\,\varepsilon_i,\qquad Z,\varepsilon_i\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,1)Ai​=ρA​​Z+1−ρA​​εi​,Z,εi​∼iidN(0,1)

Default occurs when the latent state crosses the probability-implied threshold.

Yi=1 ⁣{Ai<Φ−1(pi)}Y_i=\mathbb{1}\!\left\{A_i<\Phi^{-1}(p_i)\right\}Yi​=1{Ai​<Φ−1(pi​)}

This construction preserves each marginal PD pᵢ, while bad systematic states push many obligors across their thresholds together.

The experiment holds marginal PD fixed and compares independent with correlated portfolios. Mean loss remains about 2.25% in both. The 99.9th percentile rises from 2.99% under independence to 14.36% under one-factor dependence. Expected loss barely moves; unexpected loss and capital change by multiples.

Dependence also invalidates naive inference. A nominal 5% IID-binomial test rejects 14.96% of the time when within-cluster default correlation is 10%. An exact p-value is only exact under its assumptions. Better arithmetic cannot rescue a false probability model.

14 / Borrower clustering

Ten thousand borrowers can contain far fewer than ten thousand independent signals

Borrowers cluster through households, employers, industries, geography, collateral, originators, and origination vintages. Members of one cluster share latent shocks, so observing one default partly changes what should be believed about nearby borrowers. This is statistical clustering—dependence among labels—not merely an unsupervised algorithm that groups similar feature vectors.

Let clusters have approximately m borrowers and let ρ_c denote the intracluster correlation of the binary outcomes. Under equal cluster sizes and exchangeable within-cluster correlation, the variance of a sample mean is inflated relative to IID sampling by the design effect.

DE=1+(m−1)ρc\mathrm{DE}=1+(m-1)\rho_cDE=1+(m−1)ρc​

An approximate effective sample size divides the row count by that inflation.

neff≈nDEn_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx\frac{n}{\mathrm{DE}}neff​≈DEn​

With 10,000 borrowers, average cluster size 25, and correlation only 5%, the design effect is 2.2 and the effective sample size falls to roughly 4,545.

As an engineer, I learned to stop treating row count as sample size. The database reports storage volume; inference needs independent variation. This changes how I partition data, estimate uncertainty, size a backtest, and explain why a model trained on millions of tightly related observations may still be statistically fragile.

The practical intuition is duplication of information. When an employer fails, 25 borrower outcomes do not represent 25 unrelated macro experiments. They represent one shared shock plus idiosyncratic variation. A small positive correlation becomes economically large when multiplied by many neighbours.

Point estimation of a marginal mean can remain unbiased under cluster sampling, yet its naive standard error is too small. A cluster-robust sandwich estimator aggregates score contributions within cluster before forming variance.

V^cluster=A^−1 ⁣(∑gGgGg⊤)A^−⊤\widehat V_{\mathrm{cluster}}=\widehat A^{-1}\!\left(\sum_gG_gG_g^\top\right)\widehat A^{-\top}Vcluster​=A−1(g∑​Gg​Gg⊤​)A−⊤

Here Gg is the score summed over borrowers in cluster g and A is the observed information. The number of independent clusters—not merely rows—governs reliability, especially when clusters are few or unbalanced.

Resampling and validation must preserve the dependence unit. A borrower-level bootstrap breaks shared shocks; a cluster bootstrap resamples employers, geographies, or vintages as blocks. Cross-validation should prevent related borrowers from appearing on both sides of a fold, and the forward holdout should preserve whole time cohorts. Otherwise the test set quietly contains information already visible in training.

Cluster labels are rarely perfect. Employer, postcode, sector, and vintage capture different dependence channels, while large macro shocks cross all of them. A useful workflow reports sensitivity across plausible cluster definitions, uses multiway clustering when justified, and stress-tests residual correlation after conditioning. Clustering is a model of information overlap, not a box to tick.

15 / Monthly survival

A borrower earns the next month only by surviving the current one

A 12-month Bernoulli label collapses an entire path into one bit. Monthly survival keeps the path. At the start of month t, the risk set Rₜ contains borrowers who have not defaulted, prepaid, matured, or been censored before that month. Only members of Rₜ are eligible to default during t; everyone else has already left the experiment.

Let T denote the discrete default month and let Hₜ contain only information available at the start of month t. The monthly hazard is a conditional probability, not a cumulative probability.

ht=Pr⁡(T=t∣T≥t,Ht)h_t=\Pr(T=t\mid T\geq t,\mathcal H_t)ht​=Pr(T=t∣T≥t,Ht​)

In a default-only model, survival through month t means T > t and requires surviving every earlier conditional trial.

S(t∣H1:t)=Pr⁡(T>t∣H1:t)=∏s=1t(1−hs)S(t\mid\mathcal H_{1:t})=\Pr(T>t\mid\mathcal H_{1:t})=\prod_{s=1}^{t}\left(1-h_s\right)S(t∣H1:t​)=Pr(T>t∣H1:t​)=s=1∏t​(1−hs​)

The cumulative probability of default by t is then its complement.

PD(0,t∣H1:t)=Pr⁡(T≤t∣H1:t)=1−S(t∣H1:t)\mathrm{PD}(0,t\mid\mathcal H_{1:t})=\Pr(T\leq t\mid\mathcal H_{1:t})=1-S(t\mid\mathcal H_{1:t})PD(0,t∣H1:t​)=Pr(T≤t∣H1:t​)=1−S(t∣H1:t​)

Therefore twelve monthly hazards cannot simply be added except as a first-order small-hazard approximation.

In a borrower-month panel, each borrower contributes one Bernoulli row for every observed at-risk month in the set Oᵢ. The event indicator dᵢₜ equals one only in the default month. A borrower surviving month five crosses into the month-six risk set with updated months-on-book and possibly updated covariates. This expansion makes the likelihood explicit.

L=∏i∏t∈Oihitdit(1−hit)1−ditL=\prod_i\prod_{t\in\mathcal O_i}h_{it}^{d_{it}}(1-h_{it})^{1-d_{it}}L=i∏​t∈Oi​∏​hitdit​​(1−hit​)1−dit​

As an engineer, I learned that the risk set is a state machine, not a filter applied at the end. Every transition needs an event time, a reason, and an auditable rule for whether another borrower-month row may exist. That design prevents an account from quietly surviving after default, reappearing after prepayment, or contributing labels beyond the observation window.

Censoring is not survival. If observation ends after month seven, the borrower contributes seven known non-default months and no claim about months eight through twelve. Treating an immature account as a 12-month survivor biases default probability downward. Independent censoring permits standard survival estimators; informative censoring requires modelling, weighting, or sensitivity analysis.

Prepayment and default are competing exits. For this formulation, let T now denote the first-exit month and let J identify its cause. Each cause-specific discrete hazard is defined among accounts still event-free.

hj,t=Pr⁡(T=t,J=j∣T≥t,Ht)h_{j,t}=\Pr(T=t,J=j\mid T\geq t,\mathcal H_t)hj,t​=Pr(T=t,J=j∣T≥t,Ht​)

Event-free survival must now remove the sum of all cause-specific hazards.

S(t)=∏u=1t(1−∑jhj,u)S(t)=\prod_{u=1}^{t}\left(1-\sum_j h_{j,u}\right)S(t)=u=1∏t​(1−j∑​hj,u​)

The default cumulative incidence integrates the default hazard against that all-cause survival.

Fdefault(t)=Pr⁡(T≤t,J=default)=∑s=1tS(s−1)hdefault,sF_{\mathrm{default}}(t)=\Pr(T\leq t,J=\mathrm{default})=\sum_{s=1}^{t}S(s-1)h_{\mathrm{default},s}Fdefault​(t)=Pr(T≤t,J=default)=s=1∑t​S(s−1)hdefault,s​

Simply censoring prepayment and then reporting one minus the resulting default-only survival estimates a different hypothetical quantity when prepayment is informative.

Three clocks should be separated in credit data. Months-on-book captures seasoning, calendar month captures shared macro conditions, and origination vintage captures underwriting regime. A rising default curve can be an age effect, a recession period effect, or a weak vintage. Mixing these clocks produces an attractive curve with an ambiguous explanation.

Validation should follow the same path structure: compare observed and predicted hazards by month-on-book, reconstruct survival and cumulative incidence, backtest whole vintages forward, and report the number at risk beneath every curve. A monthly model that calibrates early months but loses the shrinking late risk set can still miss the 12-month probability materially.

16 / Numerical engineering

Preserve the mathematics inside finite-precision code

Multiplying thousands of probabilities underflows to zero even when the mathematical likelihood is positive.

L=∏ipiyi(1−pi)1−yiL=\prod_i p_i^{y_i}(1-p_i)^{1-y_i}L=i∏​piyi​​(1−pi​)1−yi​

The experiment’s naive full-data likelihood becomes zero, while the stable log-likelihood remains informative.

Lnaive=0.0whileℓstable=−2640.00L_{\mathrm{naive}}=0.0 \qquad \text{while} \qquad \ell_{\mathrm{stable}}=-2640.00Lnaive​=0.0whileℓstable​=−2640.00

Work in log space and use primitives with correct limiting behaviour: xlogy for event terms, xlog1py for survival terms, logaddexp for logistic loss, and a stable sigmoid.

As an engineer, I learned that algebraic equivalence does not imply computational equivalence. The probability product and the log-likelihood describe the same model on paper, but only one representation preserves information in finite precision. Numerical form belongs in the model specification because it can change whether a production result exists at all.

Vectorisation changes throughput, not probability theory. For the experiment, vectorised NumPy sampling is roughly 310 times faster than a Python loop on the measured hardware. Large portfolios should be processed in bounded chunks when only aggregate counts or losses are required; materialising every intermediate array is not a mark of vectorisation.

Reproducibility is an engineering feature. Parallel workers must not reuse random seeds. A master seed should spawn independent child streams, and the result should record generator algorithm, dependency versions, worker assignment, code version, and input snapshot.

Complexity must be attached to the algorithm. Direct Poisson–Binomial convolution has explicit time and memory costs.

Time=O(n2)andMemory=O(n)\mathrm{Time}=O(n^2) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathrm{Memory}=O(n)Time=O(n2)andMemory=O(n)

A correct exact algorithm can still be unusable at production scale; FFT, saddlepoint, translated-Poisson, normal, or Monte Carlo approximations require error budgets rather than blind substitution.

17 / Testing mathematics as software

A quant test suite should attack invariants, not examples

Example-based tests catch regressions in known cases. Property tests catch violations of the mathematical object. For every valid probability, Bernoulli masses must be non-negative and sum to one.

f(y;p)≥0(y∈{0,1}),f(0;p)+f(1;p)=1(p∈[0,1]).\begin{aligned}f(y;p)&\geq0 &&(y\in\{0,1\}),\\ f(0;p)+f(1;p)&=1 &&(p\in[0,1]).\end{aligned}f(y;p)f(0;p)+f(1;p)​≥0=1​​(y∈{0,1}),(p∈[0,1]).​

Simulation averages should approach the target within a tolerance derived from their exact standard deviation, not an arbitrary decimal.

SE⁡(Y‾)=p(1−p)n\operatorname{SE}(\overline Y)=\sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}}SE(Y)=np(1−p)​​

Expected loss must also be monotone in probability of default, loss given default, and exposure when all three inputs are non-negative.

Estimator tests should verify first-order conditions and recovery. At the MLE, the score is approximately zero and the Hessian is negative. Synthetic parameters should be recovered over repeated samples without systematic bias beyond Monte Carlo error. Zero-default and all-default boundaries require explicit tests because generic optimisers and logarithms fail there first.

Statistical tests require tests of their own. A level-5% test controls its worst-case null rejection probability at no more than 5%; discreteness can make the actual size smaller.

sup⁡θ∈Θ0Pr⁡θ(reject H0)≤α=0.05\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_0}\Pr_\theta(\text{reject }H_0)\leq\alpha=0.05θ∈Θ0​sup​θPr​(reject H0​)≤α=0.05

Simulate data across the null, attach a binomial Monte Carlo interval to each estimated rejection rate, and then break independence deliberately to observe size inflation. This distinguishes a code bug from an assumption failure.

As an engineer, I learned that a passing unit test is weak evidence when its tolerance was invented. The useful test begins with the mathematical distribution of the error, converts that distribution into an expected range, and then checks both the implementation and the assumptions that justified the range.

Pipeline tests defend chronology and definitions. Assert that every feature timestamp is no later than the information date, every label has a fully matured horizon, no obligor crosses development and holdout, transformations are fit only on development, and default taxonomy is versioned. These are data invariants with greater economic value than a high unit-test count.

Performance tests should state scale and budget: runtime complexity, peak memory, deterministic equivalence between scalar and vectorised paths, and stability under extreme logits. Production readiness means the mathematical contract, statistical behaviour, data lineage, and computational envelope are all testable.

18 / Forward experiment

The model is falsified precisely, not dismissed vaguely

The research hypothesis is that a development logistic model produces transportable 12-month PDs suitable for unadjusted holdout approval and expected-loss estimation. Acceptance requires holdout calibration, economically useful discrimination, expected-loss error below 10%, positive approved-book profit, and no material selection failure.

The holdout observes 477 defaults where the model expects 395.28. The standardised calibration statistic is far into the upper tail.

z=4.9316andp-value=8.156×10−7z=4.9316 \qquad \text{and} \qquad p\text{-value}=8.156\times10^{-7}z=4.9316andp-value=8.156×10−7

AUC is 0.8155 and approved-loan realised profit remains positive at 3,014 per loan, but expected-loss error is 13.7% and an approved-only development model worsens the calibration gap to −6.73%.

The evidence identifies a level problem rather than total ranking collapse. The simulator contains an omitted structural intercept shift, so fitted odds are systematically too low across holdout macro buckets. The correct response is not to discard every part of the model and not to promote it because AUC looks good.

Decision: retain the rank ordering as a useful component, reject unadjusted deployment as a calibrated PD system, fit a dynamic or recalibrated level, and require a fresh forward test. A research decision should say which claim failed, what evidence falsified it, which component remains useful, and what experiment can reverse the decision.

As an engineer, I learned that falsification should reduce scope precisely. A failed probability level does not erase useful ordering, but useful ordering does not authorise a pricing system. The deployment artifact should record which contract passed, which failed, and which transformations are permitted before the next forward test.

19 / Boundary map

Know when Bernoulli is only the inner likelihood

Discrete monthly survival retains event timing but still simplifies cure, delinquency transitions, repeated restructuring, and dynamic interventions. Multi-state and recurrent-event models may be needed when a single absorbing default state is not the operational object.

Selection changes which labels exist. Outcomes for rejected applicants are unobserved, so an approved-only model estimates risk conditional on an old policy. Population inference then requires controlled exploration, external information, causal or selection modelling, or explicit sensitivity bounds.

PD alone cannot measure loss tails, liquidity, funding, market impact, or capital. The Bernoulli variable is an atom in a larger system. Knowing exactly what it omits is evidence of mastery, not a criticism of elementary probability.
